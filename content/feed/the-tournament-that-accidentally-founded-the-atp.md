The Washington Open — now the Mubadala DC Open — began in 1969 when Arthur Ashe agreed to play on one condition: 'It has to be in an integrated area so black faces come out and watch the tennis.' Ashe and tournament co-founder Donald Dell went on to co-found the ATP with Jack Kramer and Cliff Drysdale, making Washington unofficially the first ATP tournament. That origin story is why the Mubadala DC Open carries a different weight than most 500-level events — it was the political flashpoint that created the players' union protecting professional tennis to this day.
